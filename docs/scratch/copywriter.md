# Copywriter scratch — Pass 1

Lines tried and cut, and why. Later rounds should not relitigate these.

## Hero

- **Cut: "Prototype speed, production discipline."** (from §7 hero A). It is the exact sentence a
  competitor could put on their site with the name swapped. Fails the §7 test in the brief's own
  draft copy. The specifics that replaced it (architecture, physical phones, security pass) do the
  same work and cannot be swapped.
- **Cut: "with human architecture review, real-device QA, and a security pass before anything
  reaches you"** as a single em-dashed subordinate clause. Same content, split into a second
  sentence, so the section spends zero of its em-dash budget.
- **Cut hero B** ("Everyone ships fast now. We ship fast and put our name on it."). Strong, but
  "put our name on it" is a posture, not a mechanism. A stranger cannot act on it. Kept in appendix.
- **Cut hero C** as the hero. It is the best writing of the three and the worst opening for a page
  whose job is conversion: a cold visitor learns nothing about what is sold. Its three lines were
  reworked into the floor intro, where the scene is actually in front of the reader and the lines
  finally earn their keep.
- **Cut: "AI writes a lot of our code. It doesn't get the last word."** → kept, unchanged. Tried
  "AI writes most of our code" (more accurate to §3's "across the entire build") and reverted: "a
  lot" is the claim we can defend without a percentage, and Fact Checker cannot verify "most".

## The word "slop"

- **Cut entirely.** §7 permits it once. Tried it three ways: in the hero ("you don't get slop"), in
  the gates closing, and in the proof section. All three failed the "no sentence is pure
  reassurance without a concrete noun" rule — "slop" is a word about the absence of something, so
  the sentence around it never acquires a noun. §7's own closing line, "That's the difference
  between generated software and shipped software," does the same job with two concrete nouns.
  If the Orchestrator wants it, the hero body is the only place it should go.

## Floor cards

- **Cut: "Sahib owns architecture, Tanya owns review"** stated as flat fact. §5.4 lists gate
  ownership as still open, while §7's drafted cards assign it. Kept the drafted lines and attached
  a CONFIRM rather than silently inheriting an unresolved assignment.
- **Cut: "Sahib Singh — Mobile across all four stacks"** as an em-dash label. §7 style forbids
  `WORD — fragment`. Recast as a sentence.
- **Cut: "Tanya keeps the machine honest."** Reads as flattery about a person rather than a
  description of work, and it is the sort of line that gets a floor card mistaken for a mascot
  lineup (§7's own warning).
- **Cut a numbered agent roster (01/02/03).** §9.4: structural devices must encode information;
  the agent roster is not a sequence. The four gates are, and they keep the numbering.

## Gates

- **Cut: "We don't cut corners."** Pure reassurance, no noun, survives on any competitor's site.
- **Cut: "It's not automation, it's accountability."** The "It's not X, it's Y" device is banned
  and it was doing nothing that "Four things we don't hand to an agent" doesn't already do.

## Proof

- **Cut: "trusted by thousands"** and every variant reaching for the 1K+ download figure as social
  proof. 1,000+ installs is a small honest number; dressing it up is the failure the site sells
  itself on catching. Printed plainly instead.
- **Cut every Pocket Manager privacy sentence from the default copy**, including the soft ones
  ("your data stays yours", "we don't sell anything"). §5.2: the live listing currently declares
  the opposite on three axes. A soft claim contradicting a Data Safety declaration is the same
  listing-policy risk as a hard one. Flag-ON variant carries the CONFIRM and no approved wording.

## Wedding planner

- **Cut: "Plan it with the people planning it with you."** This is share/collaboration copy in
  disguise. §5.3 forbids it and calls it the single easiest mistake to make on this page.
- **Cut: "Coming soon" and "nearly there."** No dates, no soon. The stage indicator says where it
  is; the sentence should not repeat it in vaguer words.
- **Cut: "Congratulations — now the hard part."** §5.3: don't congratulate anyone. Also chirpy.
- **Cut: "for the couple juggling four events"** → "couple" is not banned, "bride"/"groom" are, but
  the section reads better and safer with the wedding as the subject rather than the people.
- **Cut: "ad-free forever."** §5.3 requires "no ads", because ad-free reads as a paid tier.

## Person pages

- **Cut finance as Sahib's spine.** §5.4 offers both. Breadth wins because it is the thread that
  connects to the studio's pitch: four stacks plus end-to-end-with-AI is why the delivery model is
  credible. Finance is the better story but it argues for a fintech contractor, not this studio.
  Finance is kept as the closing texture line, where it lands harder for being unannounced.
- **Cut: "a systems thinker"** for Tanya. Abstract, unfalsifiable, and available to anyone. The
  recommendation quote says the same thing with evidence attached, so the quote does the work.
- **Cut any layout-implying copy for /tanya/** (headings assuming a timeline). §9.3 says a
  chronological frame misrepresents her; the copy is written so a diagrammatic layout can carry it.

## Contact

- **Cut a headline reusing "Tell us what you're building"** on /contact/. It is the home CTA; two
  identical headlines one click apart reads as a template. Contact gets its own.
- **Cut: "We'd love to hear from you."** Every word of it.

## Global

- **Cut nav label "About".** There is no about page; the person pages are named after the people,
  which is more specific and shorter.
- **Cut the persistent affordance label "Let's talk".** Banned-adjacent, and it promises a call the
  §5.1 FILL has not confirmed the studio wants.
